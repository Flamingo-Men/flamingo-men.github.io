      Top 10 trade rapes of all time

      Ranked by wins above replacement (WAR), the way baseball grades players. For every week after the deal, a player only counts if he was in his new team's starting lineup, and only in games that mattered: the regular season, plus playoff weeks while that team was still alive. His edge that week is his points minus a replacement starter at his position for that season's league size (in a 10-team league, the QB11, RB26, WR26 and TE12; kickers and defenses against the average started one). Each week's edge turns into wins using how spread out that season's weekly scores were, so 25 extra points in a week is worth about a quarter of a win. 2016 and 2017 have no saved lineups, so they're estimated from weekly stats.

      Top 5 waiver pickups of all time

      Ranked by the points the player scored in the starting lineup of the team that picked him up, from ESPN box scores (2018 to 2026). In the overall list, running backs and wide receivers count in full, tight ends at 85% and quarterbacks at 60%, because a starting QB is easy to find on waivers and an RB or WR is not. Pick a position to see its top 5 by raw points.

      Top 10 seasons of all time

      Every team-season is scored out of 100 on the whole year: regular-season record (30), how far it went in the playoffs (30 for a title, 18 for the final, 10 for the semis), roster strength (25: the best lineup’s season points over a replacement starter for that league size, against the best roster ever) and points scored (15, by rank in that season). The total is then scaled for league size, ×0.90 for 8 teams, ×0.95 for 10 and ×1.00 for 12, since a bigger league is harder to win. Rosters are the final ESPN rosters for 2018 to 2025, plus the champion and Sacko rosters on file for 2016 and 2017. Open a season to see the breakdown and the lineup.
